# Chapter 2: How to Get Your Product Mentioned in AI Answers

Buyers used to look for products in search engines, and what you competed for was a ranking. Now they just ask an AI: "Given our size and our budget, who should we pick?" — **what you have to do has become: appear in its answer, and not be described wrongly.**

This is not something money can buy. There is no ad slot inside an AI answer. All you have are the 10 methods below, to make the facts about you worth being written into the answer.

The first 4 are must-dos, the middle 3 are pitfalls to avoid, and the last 3 are advanced.

---

## Method 1: Ask AI 25 Questions a Month and See How It Describes You

**What to do**: Get clear on what AI says about you today — this is the starting point of every action.

**How**

1. Prepare 25 questions, mixing three kinds: category phrasings ("which companies do X"), feature phrasings ("which tools support Y"), and **conditional phrasings** ("we're 50 people, our budget is 2,000 yuan a month, we need Gantt charts and time tracking — which vendor should we pick"). The third kind is closest to a real buyer.
2. Ask at each of four entry points: Google (including AI Overviews), ChatGPT, Perplexity, and for Chinese business add Doubao or DeepSeek.
3. Each time record four things: **whether you were mentioned / what rank and whether it's accurate / who was cited / which competitors were in the same answer**.
4. Do it once on a fixed day each month, for three months.

**Example**: When we ran this test ourselves, we found that for the same question and the same model, asking twice in a row, the overlap of the recommended list was only 22%–56%; switching to another model, the overlap was only 4%–21%. **So a single test result is meaningless; you have to watch the trend month by month.**

**Pitfalls**: Asking only "which companies do X". That measures whether you have a name, not whether you can get into the candidate set. The field most easily missed among the four is "whether it's accurate" — **not being mentioned is only absence, but being described wrongly is lying on your behalf, and you have no way to appeal.**

## Method 2: Say the Whole Thing in the First Sentence

**What to do**: Let AI pull your content straight out and use it. It synthesizes from extracted paragraphs; it does not score whole pages.

**How**

1. The first sentence of every page and every section gives the conclusion directly, with no buildup.
2. Write specs, pricing logic, and applicable conditions in full — factual density beats keyword density.
3. Don't use pure client-side rendering: most AI crawlers do not execute JavaScript, so content that depends on browser rendering is, in their eyes, the same as not written.
4. Use list form more: **52% of listicle articles achieve high citation rates on ChatGPT** [third-party verified].

**Example**: Academic research on GEO tested 9 optimization methods; keyword stuffing was almost useless, and on Perplexity even performed **10% worse** than doing nothing [official].

**Pitfalls**: Making the page pretty but unable to yield facts. AI does not eat design; it eats sentences.

## Method 3: Publish Numbers Nobody Else Has

**What to do**: When you hold data nobody else has, that is the highest-yield move for being cited.

**How**

1. Inventory your unique numbers: product usage data, order structure, service tickets, customer surveys — anonymized, they can be published.
2. Run a small-sample customer survey once a quarter (10–20 in-depth interviews is enough), with questions picked from the ones that most often stump sales.
3. When publishing, include sample size, methodology, and time window — without these, neither AI nor readers will believe it.
4. Set aside a budget line for "data and reports": an industry-recommended split is **40% core SEO, 25% digital PR, 20% data and reports, 10% training, 5% experimentation** [third-party verified]. That last item is missing from most companies' budget sheets.

**Example**: The three best-performing methods in the GEO research — injecting statistics, injecting quotations, and citing sources — gave relative lifts of **30%–40%** [official]. What they share: **all of them require you to hold something nobody else has.**

**Pitfalls**: Repackaging an industry report and publishing it again. That is relay, not first-hand data.

## Method 4: Make the Whole Web Give One Version of the Facts

**What to do**: AI stitches its answer across sources; when the versions don't match, it decides you are "uncertain".

**How**

1. First build a single-fact sheet: full company name and abbreviation, founding date, official product name and translation, pricing definitions, order-of-magnitude of customers, certification numbers.
2. Reuse it verbatim everywhere you speak publicly: your own site, encyclopedia entries, third-party review pages, recruiting pages, product data feeds.
3. **Don't write two prices on your own site and product feed** — at the answer layer, two prices equal no price.

**Example**: Real-time data from one monitoring firm shows **51.7% of AI citations point at the brand's own website** [vendor claim]. The biggest source of citations is often yourself — provided it can be read, and the version is consistent.

**Pitfalls**: Changing only your own site and ignoring the outdated information on third-party pages. AI reads the whole web.

## Method 5: Read Your Own Server Logs

**What to do**: No tools to buy — just look at which of your pages the AI crawlers are fetching.

**How**

1. Have a technical colleague pull the AI crawlers' access logs out separately.
2. Look at two things: which pages are being fetched often (that is the raw material of your being cited), and which pages are fetched repeatedly yet never cited (their content is substandard or unclear).
3. For the second group, change how they are written, and look at the logs again a month later.

**Example**: After Glasp optimized using the log method, daily sessions from ChatGPT rose from 500 to 19,000 [third-party verified: founder's own account].

**Pitfalls**: Buying only a third-party dashboard. It sells observation, not causation — it can tell you a ranking, not whether the money was worth it.

## Method 6: Don't Buy Reviews — the FTC Really Does Fine You

**What to do**: Rule the "buying" route out entirely, and replace it with genuine third-party evidence.

**Why**

In August 2024, the U.S. Federal Trade Commission passed a rule 5–0 making six categories of conduct unlawful: fake reviews, **AI-generated fake reviews**, paying money or goods for positive reviews, insiders writing reviews without disclosure, **company-controlled review sites falsely claiming independence**, and buying and selling accounts to inflate metrics [official].

In April 2026 the first major case landed: a brand selling children's height-growth supplements touted its results while having employees write thousands of five-star reviews, trading discounts for reviews, and using bot accounts to post software-generated comments — **it was hit with a $4 million judgment** [official].

**The key point**: in this case, **false advertising and fake reviews were fined together** — fake reviews supply "social proof" for false advertising. On AI it is exactly the same: **"buying AI mentions" and "buying reviews" are the same category of thing in the regulator's eyes.**

**Pitfalls**: Thinking you can slip through because AI can't see it. The bar for reporting is lower than you think.

## Method 7: Don't Mass-Produce Template Pages — Google Will Cut You Off Overnight

**What to do**: Make the pages you "feed to AI" read as human-written, incremental content.

**Why**

The templated pages you mass-produce on your own site will hit Google's cleanup first — and it won't notify you when it happens. In one core update in March 2026, hit sites **dropped 60%–90% in ranking overnight, lost an average of 87% of traffic, and all the impact showed within 14 days; there was no manual penalty notice, no Search Console alert, and no avenue to appeal** [third-party verified].

Google explicitly named three violating practices: **mass AI pages without editorial review** (structure repeated across thousands of variants fails), **pure template variable substitution**, and **data aggregation with no added value** (scrape data and publish it directly; "surface rewriting" with AI is not enough either) [official].

**Pitfalls**: Thinking good prose gets you off the hook. What's judged is structural repetition, not whether the sentences read well.

## Method 8: Add Three More Moves for the Chinese Market

**What to do**: The Chinese corpus is different from overseas — most of what lives in WeChat Official Accounts and inside platforms is not crawled, so far less Chinese content is readable by AI.

**How**

1. **Move the facts onto public web pages**: your own site's knowledge base, industry media, platforms that can be indexed. WeChat Official Accounts drive relationship conversion, but at the answer layer they are nearly invisible.
2. **Maintain the entries correctly**: the names, qualifications, and timelines on encyclopedias and industry directories must stay consistent with public business-registration information. In the Chinese market, duplicate names are common and entities change frequently, so being mistaken for someone else is a high-frequency problem.
3. **Work the in-platform search**: Chinese buyers often compare inside on-site search on Xiaohongshu and Douyin. Title as question, first paragraph as conclusion, and serious replies in the comments are more effective than volume.

**One more ruler**: the Measures for the Labeling of AI-Generated Synthetic Content took effect on September 1, 2025: AI-generated content must declare its identity, and platforms must verify it at upload [third-party verified]. **This is protection for people doing real work** — it shuts down the route of "mass-passing content off as human-written", and by the same token raises the relative value of content that is genuine, attributed, and checkable.

## Method 9: Don't Panic When a Platform Turns "Being Cited" Into "Being Admitted"

**What to do**: Accept that a platform will tighten its entry points at any time, so keep the assets in your own hands.

**Why**

In November 2025, OpenAI launched ChatGPT "shopping research": the user states a need, it asks follow-up questions about budget preferences, compares prices across sites, and finally gives a short candidate list — **for a merchant to get onto that list, it has to go through an allowlisting application** [official].

**How**

1. Keep the factual assets in your own hands: your own site, product data, first-party data — they can be carried to another platform (Chapter 21 covers the hedging).
2. Don't bet on a single entry point: **only 11% of domains are cited by both ChatGPT and Perplexity**; ChatGPT loves citing Wikipedia (7.8% of its citations), while Google AI Overviews and Perplexity love citing Reddit [vendor claim].

## Method 10: Vet the Vendor Before You Hire One

**What to do**: Spend half an hour screening out unreliable vendors.

**How**

1. Take the vendor's name to four AIs: "How is company X?" "What cases has X done?"
2. Ask it to give **its own visibility baseline**: under which questions it gets mentioned, how the data was collected (API or sampled from the interface), and the sample size.
3. Then ask three more things: can it give executable actions, is there a control group proving the effect, and who is accountable when things go wrong.

**Example**: One monitoring firm's own data shows that **70% of marketing agencies are not cited on AI platforms themselves** [vendor claim].

**Pitfalls**: Letting the vendor prove its value with a dashboard it made itself. An industry roundup in this field ranked the roundup's own author first [vendor claim] — **a leaderboard is not due diligence.**

## Notes for this chapter

- The "up to +40%" in the GEO research was measured in a self-built experimental environment (Google takes the top 5 sources + model generation), **not on any commercial AI search product**, and what it measured was "the relative lift of the same content after improvement". Fine as a direction, not fine as a promise.
- All the "AI traffic converts better" numbers (+42%, 25× and so on) come from vendors testing and reporting on themselves, with small samples and selection bias. **The direction is credible; the multiple cannot be used as a budget assumption.**
- On the distribution of cited sources at Chinese AI entry points, there is very little publicly checkable material — this is the thinnest part of the book; Chapter 8 handles the two sets of rules.
