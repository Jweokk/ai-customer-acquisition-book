# Chapter 2: How to Get Your Product Mentioned in AI Answers

Buyers used to look for products in search engines, and what you competed for was a ranking. Now they just ask an AI: "Given our size and our budget, who should we pick?" — **what you have to do has become: appear in its answer, and not be described wrongly.**

This is not something money can buy. There is no ad slot inside an AI answer. All you have are the 10 methods below, to make the facts about you worth being written into the answer.

The first 4 are must-dos, the middle 3 are pitfalls to avoid, and the last 3 are advanced.

---

## Method 1: Ask AI 25 Questions a Month and See How It Describes You

**What to do**: Get clear on what AI says about you today — this is the starting point of every action.

**How**

1. Prepare 25 questions, mixing three kinds: category phrasings ("which companies do X"), feature phrasings ("which tools support Y"), and **conditional phrasings** ("we're 50 people, our budget is 2,000 yuan a month, we need Gantt charts and time tracking — which vendor should we pick"). The third kind is closest to a real buyer.
2. Ask at each of four entry points: Google (including AI Overviews), ChatGPT, Perplexity, and for Chinese business add Doubao or DeepSeek.
3. Each time record four things: **whether you were mentioned / what rank and whether it's accurate / who was cited / which competitors were in the same answer**.
4. Do it once on a fixed day each month, for three months.

**Example**: When we ran this test ourselves, we found that for the same question and the same model, asking twice in a row, the overlap of the recommended list was only 22%–56%; switching to another model, the overlap was only 4%–21%. **So a single test result is meaningless; you have to watch the trend month by month.** Trends have a magnitude of their own: Semrush's analysis of 230,000 prompts across 13 weekly snapshots (2025-07-14 to 2025-10-12) shows ChatGPT's citations of Reddit collapsing from about 60% of replies to about 10% in mid-September 2025, and Wikipedia from about 55% to under 20%, while Google AI Mode and Perplexity stayed roughly flat over the same weeks [third-party verified] — visibility can halve in a few weeks, so a single snapshot cannot carry a budget decision. Not everyone is even measuring: one technical-community write-up says 75% of companies have no AI visibility tracking at all [third-party verified: technical community]. Churn has a finer reading too: a report built on millions of data points says only 30% of brands stay visible from one answer to the next, and only 20% remain across five consecutive runs [vendor claim] — stability is not the default state, it is the exception for a minority of brands. Set the ruler against brand size and the ladder is explicit: an arXiv preprint tracking more than 100 brands and 100,000+ prompt responses puts global household names in about 73% of relevant AI answers on their very first tracking run, established mid-market and regional brands at about 44%, and niche or small brands at about 11% — roughly 30 percentage points per rung; about 78% of the citations it observed went to corporate websites, YouTube led among non-corporate sources, and the ranked "best-of" listicle was the most-cited content format at about 21% of all citations; sentiment is the unstable part, flipping about 6.7 times more often than whether the brand is mentioned at all [official: academic preprint]. Down at the citation level, the same kind of capture gives a different reading: 480 questions, four engines, one capture recording 16,069 citations across 7,775 distinct domains. ChatGPT cited a source in only 72 of its 480 answers, and 69 of those sit in two question types — who provides something and what it costs; on the other six types it cited nothing in any industry, while still writing answers averaging 2,303 characters. The closest engine pair, Perplexity and Google AI Overviews, overlaps at a mean per-question Jaccard of just 0.181, and when the same questions were re-asked 38 minutes later Perplexity agreed with itself at 0.900; 5,201 of the 7,775 cited domains, 67%, were cited exactly once, while sites owned by the companies that sell the thing being asked about held 54.0% of all citations [third-party verified: single capture, full dataset published]. Being cited therefore splits in two: engines prefer concrete evidence on a concrete question rather than citing the same large sites over and over.

**Pitfalls**: Asking only "which companies do X". That measures whether you have a name, not whether you can get into the candidate set. The field most easily missed among the four is "whether it's accurate" — **not being mentioned is only absence, but being described wrongly is lying on your behalf, and you have no way to appeal.** That last clause, though, was partly rewritten by the courts in 2026: a preliminary injunction from the Regional Court of Munich on 28 May held that an AI Overview, because the model compiles the information itself and condenses it into a summary, constitutes an "independent presentation" for which the platform is responsible, so the older precedent that search engines are not liable for their results does not carry over. In that case the AI mixed information about other, genuinely dubious companies into a Munich publishing group and drew connections that appeared in none of the linked sources; the court found this impaired its commercial reputation with a risk of repetition, and enjoined the statements, with fines of up to EUR 250,000 per breach [third-party verified: full judgment, English translation]. The cost of being described wrongly is shifting from "figure it out yourself" to "a claim you can assert".

## Method 2: Say the Whole Thing in the First Sentence

**What to do**: Let AI pull your content straight out and use it. It synthesizes from extracted paragraphs; it does not score whole pages.

**How**

1. The first sentence of every page and every section gives the conclusion directly, with no buildup.
2. Write specs, pricing logic, and applicable conditions in full — factual density beats keyword density.
3. Don't use pure client-side rendering: most AI crawlers do not execute JavaScript, so content that depends on browser rendering is, in their eyes, the same as not written.
4. Use list form more: **52% of listicle articles achieve high citation rates on ChatGPT** [third-party verified].

**Example**: Academic research on GEO tested 9 optimization methods; keyword stuffing was almost useless, and on Perplexity even performed **10% worse** than doing nothing [official]. Rankings and citations are no longer the same thing: an analysis of 863,000 search results pages puts the share of AI Overview citations drawn from Google's top-10 pages at 37.9%, down from 76.1% in an earlier round of research [third-party verified] — making the top ten does not mean making the answer. Most of what gets cited is not your own site either: an analysis of 40,000+ highly cited URLs across nearly 50 product categories found that about half of ChatGPT's citations are listicles, and 58% of those listicles are ranked lists [third-party verified] — whether you get cited depends heavily on whether you appear in lists somebody else writes. Structure is itself a measurable signal: the same AI-search report says 68.7% of pages cited in ChatGPT follow a clean heading hierarchy, 87% use a single H1 and nearly 80% include lists, and pages using three or more schema types are 13% more likely to be cited [vendor claim] — being cited is not luck, it is a set of structural features you can build. The gap between ranking and citation now has cross-industry baselines: an analysis of 45 million+ search keywords across 828 large B2B companies found that AI Overviews appear on nearly 50% of results pages where B2B brands rank, while the median enterprise B2B brand is cited in just 3% of the AI Overviews it is relevant for; the median company ranks for nearly 9,700 keywords, and roughly 4,500 of those trigger AI-generated answers — half of the search footprint is already covered by generative answers, while citations stay in the single digits [third-party verified: vendor research]. One industry shows the same shape: insurance benchmarks put AI Mode at 43.9% of the citations analysed in that sector and Perplexity at 33.6%, with AI Overviews appearing on 40.7% of insurance searches and 53.3% of those citations going to pages that never cracked Google's organic top 10; the same report notes that AI engines drive around 1.08% of all website visits, so treating AI as a traffic channel gets the objective wrong from the start [third-party verified].

**Pitfalls**: Making the page pretty but unable to yield facts. AI does not eat design; it eats sentences.

## Method 3: Publish Numbers Nobody Else Has

**What to do**: When you hold data nobody else has, that is the highest-yield move for being cited.

**How**

1. Inventory your unique numbers: product usage data, order structure, service tickets, customer surveys — anonymized, they can be published.
2. Run a small-sample customer survey once a quarter (10–20 in-depth interviews is enough), with questions picked from the ones that most often stump sales.
3. When publishing, include sample size, methodology, and time window — without these, neither AI nor readers will believe it.
4. Set aside a budget line for "data and reports": an industry-recommended split is **40% core SEO, 25% digital PR, 20% data and reports, 10% training, 5% experimentation** [third-party verified]. That last item is missing from most companies' budget sheets.

**Example**: The three best-performing methods in the GEO research — injecting statistics, injecting quotations, and citing sources — gave relative lifts of **30%–40%** [official]. What they share: **all of them require you to hold something nobody else has.**

**Pitfalls**: Repackaging an industry report and publishing it again. That is relay, not first-hand data.

## Method 4: Make the Whole Web Give One Version of the Facts

**What to do**: AI stitches its answer across sources; when the versions don't match, it decides you are "uncertain".

**How**

1. First build a single-fact sheet: full company name and abbreviation, founding date, official product name and translation, pricing definitions, order-of-magnitude of customers, certification numbers.
2. Reuse it verbatim everywhere you speak publicly: your own site, encyclopedia entries, third-party review pages, recruiting pages, product data feeds.
3. **Don't write two prices on your own site and product feed** — at the answer layer, two prices equal no price.

**Example**: Real-time data from one monitoring firm shows **51.7% of AI citations point at the brand's own website** [vendor claim]. The biggest source of citations is often yourself — provided it can be read, and the version is consistent. A different sample gives a different distribution: across more than 1,000,000 citations on ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Google AI Overviews, community platforms (Reddit, Quora and the like) take 52.5% and brand-owned domains 47.5% [third-party verified]. The two samples cover different ground and the shares differ by a few points, but both point at the same thing: your own pages are the single largest source. The same analysis found a threshold further upstream — 73% of sites block AI crawlers through robots.txt, CDN rules, or JavaScript rendering [third-party verified]. Before you can be cited, you have to survive being crawled. Who gets read repeatedly at the citation layer also has a public audit: an audit of U.S. ChatGPT citations says Wikipedia accounts for 13.15% and Reddit 11.97%, together more than a quarter of all citations; The Wall Street Journal, The New York Times, Bloomberg and the Financial Times do not appear in the top 20, the only U.S. business publication in the top 20 is Forbes (No. 18, 1.38%), and Reuters ranks No. 7 (2.27%) [third-party verified] — PR tier does not equal AI citation rank.

Counted per answer rather than per citation, the picture is colder: a GEO monitoring firm that analysed millions of answers across more than eight models found that 84% of AI responses do not cite a brand's own domain at all, and that models describe a company mainly from community platforms, review sites, competitor comparison pages and press coverage; the number of domains cited in one answer varies by engine, averaging 5.8 for Gemini and 27 for Grok [third-party verified: monitoring vendor research]. The writing itself divides labour, too: an observation of 165 of one publisher's own articles as cited in Copilot (data from Bing Webmaster Tools' AI Performance report; a 29-day window, 5.4K citations) found that named category guides written as “best X for Y” took 86% of the citations, while “X vs Y” and “alternatives to Z” comparison pages were never cited once; the top 10 pages accounted for 64% of sampled citations, and within that single site, word count, heading count, tables and FAQ blocks did not separate cited from uncited pages [third-party verified: single-site observation]. That runs against the common claim that comparison pages are citation magnets; the sample is one site, one engine and 29 days, so file it as a direction rather than a verdict. Source structure can be calibrated against a cross-engine count: among U.S. ChatGPT (browsing mode) citation events in January–February 2026, Wikipedia came first with 118,285 (13.15%), Reddit second with 107,680 (11.97%), OpenAI's own domain third (6.21%), and the only retailer in the top five was Walmart (2.90%) [third-party verified] — what explains citations is which few sites get read over and over, not how many sites mention you.

**Pitfalls**: Changing only your own site and ignoring the outdated information on third-party pages. AI reads the whole web.

## Method 5: Read Your Own Server Logs

**What to do**: No tools to buy — just look at which of your pages the AI crawlers are fetching.

**How**

1. Have a technical colleague pull the AI crawlers' access logs out separately.
2. Look at two things: which pages are being fetched often (that is the raw material of your being cited), and which pages are fetched repeatedly yet never cited (their content is substandard or unclear).
3. For the second group, change how they are written, and look at the logs again a month later.

**Example**: After Glasp optimized using the log method, daily sessions from ChatGPT rose from 500 to 19,000 [third-party verified: founder's own account].

**Pitfalls**: Buying only a third-party dashboard. It sells observation, not causation — it can tell you a ranking, not whether the money was worth it.

## Method 6: Don't Buy Reviews — the FTC Really Does Fine You

**What to do**: Rule the "buying" route out entirely, and replace it with genuine third-party evidence.

**Why**

In August 2024, the U.S. Federal Trade Commission passed a rule 5–0 making six categories of conduct unlawful: fake reviews, **AI-generated fake reviews**, paying money or goods for positive reviews, insiders writing reviews without disclosure, **company-controlled review sites falsely claiming independence**, and buying and selling accounts to inflate metrics [official].

In April 2026 the first major case landed: a brand selling children's height-growth supplements touted its results while having employees write thousands of five-star reviews, trading discounts for reviews, and using bot accounts to post software-generated comments — **it was hit with a $4 million judgment** [official].

**The key point**: in this case, **false advertising and fake reviews were fined together** — fake reviews supply "social proof" for false advertising. On AI it is exactly the same: **"buying AI mentions" and "buying reviews" are the same category of thing in the regulator's eyes.**

**Pitfalls**: Thinking you can slip through because AI can't see it. The bar for reporting is lower than you think.

## Method 7: Don't Mass-Produce Template Pages — Google Will Cut You Off Overnight

**What to do**: Make the pages you "feed to AI" read as human-written, incremental content.

**Why**

The templated pages you mass-produce on your own site will hit Google's cleanup first — and it won't notify you when it happens. In one core update in March 2026, hit sites **dropped 60%–90% in ranking overnight, lost an average of 87% of traffic, and all the impact showed within 14 days; there was no manual penalty notice, no Search Console alert, and no avenue to appeal** [third-party verified].

Google explicitly named three violating practices: **mass AI pages without editorial review** (structure repeated across thousands of variants fails), **pure template variable substitution**, and **data aggregation with no added value** (scrape data and publish it directly; "surface rewriting" with AI is not enough either) [official].

**Pitfalls**: Thinking good prose gets you off the hook. What's judged is structural repetition, not whether the sentences read well.

## Method 8: Add Three More Moves for the Chinese Market

**What to do**: The Chinese corpus is different from overseas — most of what lives in WeChat Official Accounts and inside platforms is not crawled, so far less Chinese content is readable by AI.

**How**

1. **Move the facts onto public web pages**: your own site's knowledge base, industry media, platforms that can be indexed. WeChat Official Accounts drive relationship conversion, but at the answer layer they are nearly invisible.
2. **Maintain the entries correctly**: the names, qualifications, and timelines on encyclopedias and industry directories must stay consistent with public business-registration information. In the Chinese market, duplicate names are common and entities change frequently, so being mistaken for someone else is a high-frequency problem.
3. **Work the in-platform search**: Chinese buyers often compare inside on-site search on Xiaohongshu and Douyin. Title as question, first paragraph as conclusion, and serious replies in the comments are more effective than volume.

**One more ruler**: the Measures for the Labeling of AI-Generated Synthetic Content took effect on September 1, 2025: AI-generated content must declare its identity, and platforms must verify it at upload [third-party verified]. **This is protection for people doing real work** — it shuts down the route of "mass-passing content off as human-written", and by the same token raises the relative value of content that is genuine, attributed, and checkable.

## Method 9: Don't Panic When a Platform Turns "Being Cited" Into "Being Admitted"

**What to do**: Accept that a platform will tighten its entry points at any time, so keep the assets in your own hands.

**Why**

In November 2025, OpenAI launched ChatGPT "shopping research": the user states a need, it asks follow-up questions about budget preferences, compares prices across sites, and finally gives a short candidate list — **for a merchant to get onto that list, it has to go through an allowlisting application** [official].

**How**

1. Keep the factual assets in your own hands: your own site, product data, first-party data — they can be carried to another platform (Chapter 21 covers the hedging).
2. Don't bet on a single entry point: **only 11% of domains are cited by both ChatGPT and Perplexity**; ChatGPT loves citing Wikipedia (7.8% of its citations), while Google AI Overviews and Perplexity love citing Reddit [vendor claim].

## Method 10: Vet the Vendor Before You Hire One

**What to do**: Spend half an hour screening out unreliable vendors.

**How**

1. Take the vendor's name to four AIs: "How is company X?" "What cases has X done?"
2. Ask it to give **its own visibility baseline**: under which questions it gets mentioned, how the data was collected (API or sampled from the interface), and the sample size.
3. Then ask three more things: can it give executable actions, is there a control group proving the effect, and who is accountable when things go wrong.

**Example**: One monitoring firm's own data shows that **70% of marketing agencies are not cited on AI platforms themselves** [vendor claim]. The abuse risk on the provider side has already been named in an industry document: a GEO trusted-ecosystem report from the China Artificial Intelligence Industry Development Alliance (AIIA) groups the poisoning tactics into two stages — content writing and distribution — covering forged authority endorsements, smearing competitors, malicious keyword stuffing, implicit prompt injection, coordinated matrix-account posting, and contamination of high-authority sources; its governance path is written as provider self-discipline plus whole-chain coordination, and it records that the Cyberspace Administration of China has launched a special campaign, that the alliance has issued a GEO credibility self-discipline pledge with nearly 20 companies taking part, and that the Basic Requirements for Trustworthy GEO Services has been drafted and entered pilot evaluation [official: industry alliance]. When you vet a provider, read these before the performance numbers on a case-study page. The field now has its first public sizing as well: China's narrowly defined GEO services market is estimated at RMB 4.2–4.6 billion for 2026, with the top ten providers holding only about 9.8% combined share. The white paper defines mention rate, first-recommendation rate and coverage rate as the core metrics (as opposed to impressions or post volume), lists keyword stuffing, homogenized rewriting, corpus poisoning, forged authority signals and commercial disparagement as the 2026 governance targets, and admits that its own industry pain point is "passing off POC-sized or SEO work as GEO results" [third-party verified: vendor white paper, includes self-reported data].

**Pitfalls**: Letting the vendor prove its value with a dashboard it made itself. An industry roundup in this field ranked the roundup's own author first [vendor claim] — **a leaderboard is not due diligence.**

## Notes for this chapter

- The "up to +40%" in the GEO research was measured in a self-built experimental environment (Google takes the top 5 sources + model generation), **not on any commercial AI search product**, and what it measured was "the relative lift of the same content after improvement". Fine as a direction, not fine as a promise.
- All the "AI traffic converts better" numbers (+42%, 25× and so on) come from vendors testing and reporting on themselves, with small samples and selection bias. **The direction is credible; the multiple cannot be used as a budget assumption.**
- On the distribution of cited sources at Chinese AI entry points, there is very little publicly checkable material — this is the thinnest part of the book; Chapter 8 handles the two sets of rules.

- The measured numbers in CAICT's Generative Engine Optimization Industry White Paper (conversion up 2.8× in AI recommendation scenarios after GEO commercialisation, decision cycles 40% shorter) exist only as media relays today; the white paper's sample size and statistical basis are not public [third-party verified: media relay]. Fine as a direction, not as a promise.
