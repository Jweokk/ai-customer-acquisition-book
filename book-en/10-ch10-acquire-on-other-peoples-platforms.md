# Chapter 10: Acquiring Customers on Other People's Platforms: App Stores, In-Platform E-Commerce, and Industry Directories

When a customer wanted to buy something, it used to be that you pulled him in from outside; now he first searches in the app store, browses inside the e-commerce platform, and filters in the industry directory — and whichever position you hold in that venue is roughly the position at which he meets you.

For many businesses, customers are pushed out to you inside the platform rather than searched out. So this chapter gives you 5 methods to occupy your position inside the platform.

---

## Method 1: Read the platform's official rules before you list

**What to do**: Before you touch a title, change an image, or sign up for a campaign, confirm which actions will get your product or app taken down.

**How**

1. If you list an app, read Apple's App Review Guidelines first. That document is written by the platform itself and says which apps are refused and under what circumstances an app already listed gets removed. Copy out the clauses relevant to your category and keep them in a separate file — do not count on looking them up on the day you submit [official].
2. If you sell on e-commerce platforms, bookmark the Taobao Rules Center (Chinese: 淘宝规则中心) and the JD Merchant Help Center (Chinese: 京东商家帮助中心) and use them as your entry points for checking rules. These two are the general entry points to the rules; the individual clauses must be located item by item against the action you are about to take — changing a price, joining a campaign, writing promotional copy — and you must not treat someone else's screenshot or the wording forwarded in a group chat as the basis [official].
3. Book a takedown as a customer-acquisition cost. An app removed, a product removed, a store docked points — each equals losing your gateway on that platform the same day. Losing the gateway is far worse than a drop in ranking: when your ranking falls, someone scrolling further down can still find you; when the gateway is gone, nobody can reach you at all [our judgment].
4. Turn every line you have crossed into a checklist: the left column says "action," the right column says "what the rule says." Run through it item by item before every new listing, every price change, and every campaign sign-up, and submit only once it passes. The checklist beats keeping it in your head, because it is still there after the person is gone.
5. Name one person responsible for this, and put the responsibility on a name rather than writing "everyone checks." The clauses are scattered across different pages, and with nobody responsible it gets dragged out until the day something breaks, when someone opens it for the first time.
6. Check quarterly whether the rules have been updated, and revise the checklist while you are at it. Platforms change rules without notice, and after a change the answer you read last time may no longer hold.

**Example**: In 2020 Fortnite was removed from the App Store by Apple for routing around in-app purchase; Apple's public statement was that it violated App Store rules applied "equally to every developer" [third-party reported: citing Apple's statement]; after Apple enforced the new game-license rules for the China region in July 2020, monitoring by Qimai Data (Chinese: 七麦数据) recorded that on August 1 alone, within half a day, 29,800 apps were removed, 26,568 of them games [third-party reported: citing third-party monitoring]. Store rules have been rewritten too: on September 14, 2026 Apple updated the App Store Review Guidelines and reworked section 4.3 on "spam and duplicate content," naming over-saturated categories such as flashlights, sound effects, static wallpapers, basic timers and Chinese-zodiac fortune-telling apps — new submissions in those categories are rejected unless they can demonstrate unique functionality, an automated sweep of existing listings removes products with no recent updates, no active interaction and weak ratings from the public shelf, and teams that repeatedly submit reskinned duplicate apps face cross-account enforcement [third-party verified: relaying the platform's revised guidelines]. The tightening is not a single notch: Apple's updated 4.3(b) states outright that apps indistinguishable from widely available products must not be submitted, that opportunistically producing variants of existing categories or popular apps lowers App Store discovery efficiency and can lead to removal, and that repeated submissions may get a developer removed from the program [third-party verified]; in the same revision 4.1(c) bars using another party's icon, brand or product name in an app icon or name without permission, and 5.1.2(i) requires clear disclosure of where personal data is shared with third parties — explicitly including third-party AI — with the user's explicit permission obtained first [official: review guidelines].

**Pitfalls**: Treating the rules page as a first-aid kit you open only when something has gone wrong. Reading it once before listing is cheap; being taken down and starting over costs far more.

## Method 2: Fill in every field that can be searched

**What to do**: Fill in every field the platform uses for search — title, subtitle, keywords, tags, category — without missing one, and fill each accurately.

**How**

1. Confirm from the official documentation first which fields take part in search. Apple gives "App Store search" a page of its own that sets out the mechanism of in-store search and the keyword field separately [official]. The field list follows the official documentation; a table put together by a third party is only a lead.
2. Lay the fields out in a table: the left column the field name, the right column what you currently put there. Fill in the blanks and turn anything vague into something specific. This table lasts a long time once it is built, and it doubles as your master copy inside the platform.
3. Do not pile the same word into several fields. A word repeated over and over occupies the place of other words; you are narrowing your own coverage.
4. The title and subtitle answer "what I sell"; the keyword field answers "how customers will search for me." These are two different jobs, and writing them as one makes both inaccurate: stuff keywords into the title and customers cannot read it; write the keywords as a product slogan and the platform cannot match them.
5. Once it is filled in, search again from the customer's side: use the few words a customer would type and see whether your own product is still in the results. Once a month is enough; record each result in the same table and watch the change — a single result proves nothing.
6. One set of fields per platform; do not copy the same copy across every platform. Each platform searches different positions and lets you fill in different things.

**Example**: Apple gives "App Store search" a page of its own to explain in-store search and the keyword field; this is the most direct primary source for "fields take part in search" [official]. The official Chinese-side numbers make the size of this channel concrete: Douyin's official e-commerce learning center says 80% of monthly active users run searches, searching about 100 times a month on average, with 140 million e-commerce searchers per day and close to 500 million search page views per day, and that search converts 90% more efficiently than other in-feed scenarios [official: platform-reported]. The same shift appears in app stores: Google Play version 49.3 put Ask Play onto the app detail page — a Gemini-driven chatbot that answers using app metadata and user reviews, so the page now has to be written for people and for AI systems at once [third-party verified]. Field rules can also be rewritten unilaterally, and China is subtracting and adding at the same time: Douyin e-commerce's 2026 "nine merchant support policies" say the platform saved merchants over RMB 32 billion in the past year through product-card and category commission waivers, cheaper shipping insurance and a small-merchant support fund; orders generated through Qianchuan Chengfang have their technical service fee cut to 0.6%, with waivers extended to all categories and from shelf product cards into content scenarios; dynamic AIGC creative capability is claimed to generate 1–2 million assets a day; and by the end of 2025 the seeding incentive programme had drawn 3.67 million creators, with daily GMV from "search and pay after watching a video" above RMB 100 million [vendor claim: platform policy notice].

**Pitfalls**: Thinking that pretty content gets you promoted. The platform has to be able to take you in and retrieve you before ranking enters the picture at all; with the fields incomplete, none of the rest happens.

## Method 3: On the Chinese side, treat mini programs and WeChat Search as separate gateways and do each on its own

**What to do**: Inside WeChat, "the mini program being found" and "the content being indexed by WeChat Search" are two different things; do each of them separately.

**How**

1. On the mini-program side, start with the official Mini Program Search Optimization Guide; it addresses exactly how a mini program is found and how it is indexed [official]. Work through the basics according to that guide before thinking about other moves; with the basics not solid, the effort spent later is easily wasted.
2. Getting into WeChat Search is another track. The Content Integration page in the WeChat Open Documentation explains how content is integrated into WeChat Search [official]. Content that is not integrated has no share in WeChat Search, and a well-built mini program cannot make up for it.
3. The mini program's name, description, and page titles are positions to fill completely and accurately as the guide requires — do not leave them blank and do not string them together out of a pile of symbols. These are the positions where people inside WeChat recognize you.
4. Treat "can I be found inside WeChat" as its own check item, test it on its own, and test it again after a while. Do not look at it together with the results from Baidu (Chinese: 百度) or an app store: the three places do not share rules, and experience that works in one often does not hold in another.
5. There is more than one path to reach you inside WeChat: the mini program, official-account content, and WeChat Search. The gateways differ, so record them separately — that way, when something breaks, you know which path it was.
6. On the Chinese side, do not roll everything out at once. Pick one gateway and get it to the point of being findable and indexed before opening a second, or none of them will be solid.

**Example**: Independent gateways on the Chinese government-services side already have a working example: after launch, "Yue Sheng Shi" (Chinese: 粤省事) accumulated 17.67 million verified real-name users and supported 300 million visits; according to Digital Guangdong's (Chinese: 数字广东) official website, as of November 2024 real-name registered users had reached 192 million, the app had 52.13 million cumulative installs, and the official account had 38.84 million followers [third-party reported / official: platform-reported]. The official account of the store side is more complete: Tencent Marketing's FAQ lists WeChat Store's five core operating scenarios as Channels live and short video, article product cards in official and service accounts, affiliate creator selling, gifting, and WeChat Search; its method is to map the three path legs — where this person first meets you, where they order, and where you can still find them afterwards — and it states that advertising is an amplifier, not an engine [official].

**Pitfalls**: Thinking that once the mini program is built it will naturally be found inside WeChat. Being findable comes after indexing, and indexing has an official method of its own.

## Method 4: Separate the positions that are bought from the ones that are pushed

**What to do**: Before you act, get clear on the position you want to occupy: is it an ad slot bought with money, or a natural position the algorithm pushes out?

**How**

1. List the positions you want one by one, and tag each with an origin: bought (paid slots, auction slots, campaign placements) or pushed (search results, recommendation feeds, rankings). When you tag, write down plainly who you see occupying that position — do not go by impression.
2. Bought positions are accounted for as delivery: how much budget, what unit price, how long it lasts, and what is left after you stop. This kind disappears when you stop and is essentially the same thing as advertising, so it belongs in the delivery books.
3. Pushed positions are accounted for by rules: what the platform's search and recommendation rules allow and forbid. This kind gives you no chance to bid; all you can do is satisfy every condition the rules require — fields complete, content submitted in the right format, category assigned correctly.
4. Do not use the same set of actions against both kinds. Do not go rewrite copy for a problem money can solve; and for a problem the rules decide, money will not necessarily move it. When you truly cannot tell them apart, ask one question first: "who gave this position up to whom?"
5. Which kind to do first once they are separated is decided by your cash flow and your patience; do not start both at once. If cash flow is tight, do the pushed kind first — it is cheap but slow to show results; if you have room and cannot wait that long, use bought positions to run tests [our judgment].
6. Update the position table once a month. Positions on a platform move: a slot that cost money last month may become one you earn through the rules this month, and the reverse as well.

**Example**: Lalamove's (Chinese: 货拉拉) books keep "volume that was bought" and "what is left" clearly apart: its sales-expense ratio fell from 79.7% in 2021 to 10.2% in 2024, and its operating-expense ratio fell from 122.2% to 34.1% over the same period — once the part that traded subsidies for volume left the stage, what held it up was the fact that "the truck with the decal had become practically synonymous with same-city freight" [third-party verified: citing prospectus].

**Pitfalls**: Treating a "pushed position" as one "money can buy", then finding after the money is gone that the position has not moved at all.

## Method 5: Treat your position in the platform as rented, not as an asset

**What to do**: Assume by default that your ranking, your slots, and your indexing are borrowed, and plan as if they could be taken back at any moment.

**How**

1. Do not write platform ranking into the brand-assets column. Ranking is given by the platform and changes every time the platform changes a rule; book it as an owned asset and your decisions will overrate your own foundation.
2. Every plan whose results rest on a platform position gets one sentence added to it: "if this position is gone, which path do we take next?" Writing that sentence forces you to keep an option elsewhere instead of pressing all your strength onto one gateway.
3. Assign someone to watch the rules. The rules pages of the Taobao Rules Center and the JD Merchant Help Center get updates, and app review rules change too, and when a rule changes the positions can change with it [official]. The watching itself is simple: go in on a fixed date and scan, note down any update, and attach which section was changed.
4. With the same batch of customers, keep their contact details and your list of past customers in your own hands as far as possible. Treat your position in the platform only as the stretch of road that leads them out; once the road ends, the person has to land somewhere you can still reach them.
5. Keep at least one position that does not depend entirely on the platform. It may not perform best, but it is the reason you can still open the door on the day the platform changes its rules.
6. Run the platform-dependency question as a check of its own; the method sits in Chapter 21 and should not be improvised here. The check is to list every gateway and mark clearly who has the final say over each one.

**Example**: In Amazon's 2021 crackdown, the store of Mpow, the main brand of Patoson (Chinese: 帕拓逊), was banned, 606 best-selling products were taken down, and a large amount of funds was frozen; Xinghui (Chinese: 星徽股份) announced that some stores of three of its brands — RAVPower, Taotronics, and VAVA — had been suspended from selling; Caixin (Chinese: 财新) reported that the sweep involved several leading South China sellers including Patoson, Aukey (Chinese: 傲基), and Sunvalley (Chinese: 泽宝) [third-party reported]. Zynga, for its part, wrote in a filing it submitted to the SEC that a deterioration in its relationship with Facebook would hurt its business [third-party reported: citing company filing]. This channel has been falsified once already: in July 2026 Doubao, Qwen and Yuanbao all took down their user-built agent marketplaces, and the post-mortem gives the contrast — Doubao's marketplace accumulated about 8 million user-built agents against roughly 200 million daily actives, but cost RMB 130–240 million a day while earning under RMB 1 million a day in revenue; of the roughly 3 million GPTs created, only about 159,000 are public and active, and the median creator earns USD 0–100 a month [third-party verified]. Easy to list, zero distribution, negative unit economics for the individual developer — the most complete demonstration yet that the slot is rented.

**Pitfalls**: Treating a position the platform gave you as an achievement of your own. It is rented, and neither its expiry nor a change of terms comes with advance notice.

## Notes for this chapter

- Apple does not disclose its ranking algorithm, so this chapter writes only the boundaries of the rules and the field practices, and no "ranking formula." If someone hands you a ranking formula, ask for the source first [our judgment].
- The Taobao Rules Center and the JD Merchant Help Center are both general entry points to the rules, and the specific clauses must be located item by item by topic. This chapter reaches only the level of the general entry point and cites no specific clause [our judgment].
- The official rule entries for Douyin Mall (Chinese: 抖音商城) and Douyin E-Commerce (Chinese: 抖音电商) have no verifiable clause page at present, so this chapter does not cite their clauses. If you need them, go to the rules entry point yourself and locate them by topic; do not treat a relayed article as the clause [our judgment].
- For industry directories such as G2 and Capterra, no official explanation of the inclusion mechanism could be found, and Capterra's inclusion-rules page could not be verified either. In this chapter these two serve only as leads, not as a basis [our judgment].
- Numbers such as "the share of new customers inside the platform" have no verifiable source and are not written here. That is to say, Method 4 gives a classification framework; there is no platform-published ratio to cite [our judgment].
