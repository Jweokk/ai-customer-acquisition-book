# Chapter 1: See the Battlefield First — How Buyers Pick Vendors Now

Where buyers pick vendors has changed: in the past they had to try one keyword after another in a search box, collect business cards one by one at a trade show, and line you up against a few others in a table; now they often have the list settled before they ever meet you, and you have no idea how they settled it.

Today's buyers ask an AI first, read what others say first, and draw up a shortlist themselves first — and you cannot see any of these steps. So this chapter gives you 5 methods, starting with an entry-point audit, to find out where you do and do not stand.

---

## Method 1: Pin Down Where the Buyer Has Already Finished Comparing You

**What to do**: Split the buyer's path — from "the first flicker of interest" to "the first time they meet you" — into three segments, and see which steps they have already completed on their own side.

**How**

1. Cut the three segments like this: interest (they hand the whole need, as a sentence, to an AI), first screen (they get a first list with names and comparisons), and settling (they rank them in their head and decide whether to meet you). For each segment, write one sentence: "at this moment, who is deciding on their behalf."
2. Mark, in each segment, one step where "you used to have a handhold but it has now been taken away": need translation — they used to have to break their own idea into keywords; side-by-side comparison — they used to have to page through candidates themselves; the click-through — they used to have to at least open you. Once these steps are gone, the position you used to hold stops working too.
3. Break "AI entry point" down one more level, or your actions will land in the wrong place: the ones where you ask and it answers (ChatGPT, Doubao (Chinese: 豆包), Kimi and the like); the ones you never ask that it hands to you anyway (on-site search and recommendations inside Xiaohongshu (Chinese: 小红书) and Douyin (Chinese: 抖音), product recommendations on Taobao (Chinese: 淘宝) and JD.com (Chinese: 京东)); and the ones where it acts on the buyer's behalf (price comparison, form filling, ordering). The first two need watching now; the last has only landed in a narrow way so far, but keep one eye on it. The phrase "AI entry point" is too coarse — coarse enough that it makes people push hard on the wrong spot.
4. Circle the step for which there is no record of any kind. A conversation that leaves no link parameters, never enters the CRM, and shows up in no attribution report is the dark funnel — it has always existed in the trade-show bar, the industry group chat, the friend's recommendation, only now it has a form you can go back and check: the words he said to the AI, you can go ask them yourself, and see whom the model mentioned and how it relayed you.
5. This one page is written by the marketing team and updated once a quarter. Every entry-point action that follows finds its own place against it.

**Example**: In Pew Research Center's July 2025 analysis (900 U.S. adults who agreed to share their browsing behavior), when an AI summary appeared in the search results, 8% of users clicked a traditional link, versus 15% when there was no summary; only 1% of the links cited inside the summary were clicked; and about 88% of that month's summaries cited three or more sources [third-party verified].

**Pitfalls**: Thinking this is a problem AI alone created — the phrase "dark social" was already in use in 2012; what has changed is that it now starts earlier, has a wider blast radius, and you can partly see it.

## Method 2: Ask AI 25 Questions Every Month and Turn "Are We Present?" Into a Table

**What to do**: Using one fixed set of buyer questions, record "what AI says about us now" every month into a table you can compare.

**How**

1. Fixed question set: assemble 20–30 buyer phrasings from three places — the questions sales was asked most in the last three months, the comparison questions in lost-deal reviews, and the selection doubts that recur in pre-sales notes. Do not rewrite them into marketing language. Ten core ones get tested monthly, the whole set quarterly.
2. Ask at three levels, a third each: category level ("which companies do X"), feature level ("which X tools support Y"), and scenario level ("what should we choose in our situation", carrying the constraints — size, budget, industry, compliance requirements, and existing systems). The scenario level is closest to a real decision, and the quickest to expose a blank.
3. Fix the entry points; don't ask these three this month and three others next month. Overseas at least Google (including AI Overviews), ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude; in Chinese at least Doubao, DeepSeek, Kimi, Tencent Yuanbao (Chinese: 腾讯元宝). For buyers who compare inside content platforms, put on-site search on Xiaohongshu and Douyin in a separate group — do not blend it into one curve with AI visibility.
4. Each time record only four fields: whether you were mentioned; position and phrasing (what rank, and whether it is positive, neutral, negative, or got your specs and price wrong); the cited sources (your own site, third-party media, review sites, communities — or no citation at all); and side-by-side competitors (who else was in the same answer, and in what order).
5. If any one of these three things happens, the table is worth watching monthly: organic traffic other than brand terms is falling while your rankings barely move; sales gets stumped in a meeting by an AI comparison table the buyer brought, and you don't know how that comparison was produced; or you see yourself recommended in one test and strike the whole matter off the agenda.
6. Sign off on a conclusion on the stability rate alone: of these 25 questions, how many have mentioned you steadily for three straight months, with correct phrasing and a checkable third-party source. That single ratio is the conclusion you want.

**Example**: In our own measurement we used 6 real procurement questions (CRM, MES, study-abroad agencies), asked each across 5 model channels twice, and got 51 valid answers: asking the same model the same question twice, the overlap of the shortlists was only 22%–56%; switching to another model, it fell to just 4%–21% [our own measurement]. So being recommended in a single test may be nothing but sampling noise.

**Pitfalls**: Don't use an improvement in the ranking ledger to prove the citation ledger is healthy — "our search rankings are still rising" measures words, whereas on the AI side what is measured is questions; the two ledgers cannot cancel each other out.

## Method 3: Run Funnel Archaeology with Newly Closed Customers

**What to do**: Do a structured follow-up with closed customers whose memory is still fresh, and reconstruct the path they walked before they met you.

**How**

1. The respondents are not prospects but customers who just signed: do it within 2–4 weeks of signing, pick 20–30 of them, one session each, asked by sales or customer success themselves — do not outsource it into a questionnaire.
2. Ask in sequence, don't merge it into one sentence: When did this first enter your head? What did you open first — a search engine, an AI, some group, or did you ask a person? When you handed the question to the AI, how did you phrase it exactly (ask them to repeat the exact sentence, don't summarize it for them)? Which names came up in that answer? Where else did you later fill in information? Which name did you cross off, and why?
3. What you write down is not the answers but the path. Stack 20–30 of these and you get a dark-funnel map of your own industry: which entry point buyers most often start from, who keeps appearing on the shortlist, who gets crossed off, and which version of "the information about us" each of them saw.
4. Copy the customer's exact words into the question ledger (see Method 4) verbatim — do not turn them into your own summary. These raw quotes are the best source of content topics and sales scripts.
5. Run one round every quarter, with a fresh batch of newly closed customers each round.

**Example**: 6sense's 2025 B2B Buyer Experience Report, a survey of nearly 4,000 B2B buyers worldwide, says 95% of winners were already on the "day-one list", and about four-fifths of deals are won by a preferred vendor before any contact [vendor claim].

**Pitfalls**: Don't ask one "how did you hear about us?" and call it done — customers' recall is neither accurate nor free of hindsight rationalization; what you want to ask is what exactly they opened at the time, and how they put it in their own words.

## Method 4: Turn Buyers' Exact Wording Into a Question Ledger

**What to do**: Record every question a buyer has asked, in their own words, as an asset that keeps updating.

**How**

1. At minimum these fields: the question as asked; the source of the question (AI session / sales call / support ticket / Douyin DM / community); the asker's role; the buying stage; whether it involves a competitor comparison; the material used in the answer; and whether it affected the close.
2. Two sets of sources: overseas ones come mainly from call recordings, follow-ups on review sites, and forum posts; on the Chinese side add Douyin DMs, WeCom conversations, community questions, and support chats — these are both a source of questions and an entry point for handling them.
3. Who records: sales and support jot them down as they go, and marketing collects once a month, de-duplicates, classifies, and marks which questions currently have no piece of content that can answer them.
4. Three uses: a source of topics for content and material; a real script for sales training; and a substitute observation when the visible funnel data is distorted — the structure of the questions and changes in the volume of asking often reflect a shift in demand earlier than the reports do.
5. Archive once a month, look at structural change once a quarter.

**Example**: Superhuman clustered users' own words and fed them back into copy and feature prioritization [third-party: founder-authored account]; Microsoft's Customer Connection program has engineering teams collect customer habits, pain points, and suggestions directly, and change the product accordingly [official].

**Pitfalls**: Don't turn it into a support-ticket tally — what makes the ledger valuable is the question as asked; rewriting the question into your own classification label deletes the most valuable part.

## Method 5: Tell "AI Products" Apart From "AI Models" — Don't Measure with the Wrong Ruler

**What to do**: Before you start testing, tell apart whether you are testing an AI product with live retrieval, or a bare model — the two do not measure the same thing.

**How**

1. The product layer is what the customer uses: ChatGPT Search, Perplexity, Google's AI Overviews and AI Mode, domestic assistants with retrieval. Their answers carry cited sources; this layer measures citations — who got cited, and how many times.
2. The model layer is calling the API directly with retrieval off: the answer relies on the model's memory, and this layer measures the list — who it remembers, and at what rank. With a technical team on hand, this is the layer people most easily test as a side effect, but it is not the thing in front of your customer.
3. Record the two numbers separately; do not merge them into one curve: the product layer looks at cited sources and the accuracy of phrasing, the model layer looks at shortlist overlap and stability rate.
4. On a limited budget, test the product layer first — that is the thing actually in the customer's hands; keep the model layer as a control, to see how your position on the list shifts in the model's memory.
5. When reporting upward, say clearly which layer you tested. Using a bare model's result to "confirm you were recommended" is just self-comfort.

**Example**: Across the 51 valid answers at the bare-model layer in our own measurement, 0 carried a clickable source [our own measurement]; while an analysis of more than 24,000 conversation groups and more than 366,000 embedded citations in AI Search Arena shows that at the product layer the sources are highly concentrated, with news citations at about 9% and concentrated in the hands of a few outlets [official: academic preprint].

**Pitfalls**: Don't use one layer's conclusion to judge the other — the layer you tested may not be the layer your customer uses.

## Notes for this chapter

- The click and traffic data above is about "whether you can be seen", which is not the same as a closed deal. Public research offers no citable quantitative conclusion on the causal strength between being cited and closing, and our own measurement cannot answer it either — the citation ledger is positioned as a qualification metric, not a revenue metric [our judgment].
- The same fact comes in many percentage versions on the market ("buyers complete most of the purchasing journey before contacting sales" has claims ranging from under 20% to over 90%); the differences come from the year, how the journey is defined, what "contacting sales" means, and which step "complete" refers to. Break the definition apart before citing; don't pick a handy one [our judgment].
- SparkToro's zero-click-rate series changed data sources three times across the years (Jumpshot in 2016 and 2019, Datos in 2024); this book cites it only by "year + data source" and does not join years from different sources into one curve; the U.S. zero-click rate for January–April 2026 was 68.01% [third-party verified].
- Gartner's "search engine volume will drop 25% by 2026" is a forecast published in February 2024, not a measured result; this book labels it "forecast" throughout and does not use it as a factual baseline [official: forecast].
- Overseas samples cannot be extrapolated to Chinese. Pew's sample is a browsing panel of 900 U.S. adults, and 6sense's sample is B2B buyers in English-speaking markets; on the Chinese side there is no equally rigorous quantitative study of "buyers researching with AI". CNNIC's figure for generative-AI user scale only shows how big the entry point is, not the behavior — and that number comes relayed through a vendor press release, so this book did not verify the original report item by item [third-party verified: relayed via vendor press release].
