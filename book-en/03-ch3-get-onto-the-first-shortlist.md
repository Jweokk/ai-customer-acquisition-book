# Chapter 3: Get Onto the Buyer's First Shortlist

Before a buyer ever contacts you, they have already drawn up a shortlist of three to five vendors — if you are not on it, your product, your price, your cases, and your pitch have not yet earned the right to take the field. Buyers shortlist three to five names the moment they start researching, and that shortlist is the starting point for every price comparison that follows. So this chapter gives you 6 methods to make "you're on it" the default.

---

## Method 1: Turn "Did It Mention Me?" Into a Record Sheet You Can Read

**What to do**: Using fixed questions, multiple entry points, and repeated rounds, measure where you actually stand on that shortlist today.

**How**

1. Pick 3–5 whole sentences a buyer would really ask, not keywords. One set in Chinese and one in English.
2. Ask each question at 3 entry points, 3 rounds each. Write the entry points as specific products, and note whether each one is a retrieval-backed product form or a bare model: ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Perplexity, Doubao, Tencent Yuanbao, DeepSeek.
3. Each time record six columns: the question as asked / entry point and whether it has retrieval / round / appearance status (absent, present but undescribed, present and described correctly, present but described wrongly) / same-list entities (who else was in this answer) / stability share.
4. Do it on a fixed day each month, three months running.
5. If any one of three things is missing, the conclusion doesn't hold: repeat the same question over multiple rounds to measure stability, run several entry points side by side to measure entry-point dependence, and ask once in each language to measure the language partition.

**Example**: We ran a round of multi-model testing: 6 real procurement questions (3 Chinese/English pairs, covering CRM, MES, study-abroad agencies) × 5 model channels × 2 repeats per question, giving 51 valid answers; for the same question and the same model, asking twice in a row, the shortlist overlap was only 22%–56%, and switching to another model, the pairwise overlap was only 4%–21% [our own measurement]. Marketing has long agreed on the candidate set: since Howard & Sheth proposed the "evoked set" in 1969, two-stage purchasing has been the basic framework; Andrews & Srinivasan added half of the methodology in 1995 — the consideration set need not rely on consumers' self-reports but can be estimated in reverse from scanner panel data, and ignoring the consideration-set stage systematically misestimates choice [official].

**Pitfalls**: Treating one run's result as a conclusion. At a drift of 22%–56%, one chance appearance makes you think you can close the project and pull the resources, and one chance absence makes you abandon a position that is merely fluctuating.

## Method 2: Watch One Number Only — the Stability Share

**What to do**: Change the criterion from "were we recommended?" to "how stable is our position within the distribution?"

**How**

1. Compute this number: stability share = the number of times you appear across a fixed question set × fixed entry points × N repeated rounds ÷ the total number of collections.
2. Ask one question 9 times (3 entry points × 3 rounds) and you appear 2 times; the stability share is about 22%. That number is not necessarily bad — it says you hold an unstable position in the distribution, and the next step is to see what is propping that position up.
3. It only counts if it is "present and described correctly". Not being mentioned is only absence; being described wrongly is lying on your behalf, and you have no way to appeal.
4. Look at the "same-list entities" column on its own: if the same list is always the same set of names, the contexts in which you are remembered are too narrow, and you need to widen them; if the same list differs every time, entity consistency is fine and you should keep consolidating the facts.
5. Watch the trend by month, not the number on a given day.

**Example**: The popular claim that "AI only recommends three to five candidates" does not hold in our measurement: the median number of recommended entities in a single answer is about 6–10, the longest is more than 15, and the union of the four models' lists reaches 43 entities [our own measurement]. What is genuinely scarce is being mentioned stably and repeatedly across multiple entry points. The product layer (AI search with live retrieval) is indeed highly concentrated: an analysis of more than 24,000 conversation groups, 65,000 answers, and more than 366,000 embedded citations in AI Search Arena found that the citations in AI answers are long dominated by a few sources, and that the quality of the cited source has no significant effect on user satisfaction — users neither know nor care whom the AI put on the list [official (academic preprint, before peer review)].

**Pitfalls**: Using "we were recommended" as the reason to launch a project. The signal that justifies a project is that two of three entry points do not have you.

## Method 3: Tie Your Name to the Buying Situation

**What to do**: Write "brand name × the sentence the buyer uses to describe the need" into every piece of material — don't just make people remember your name.

**How**

1. List your "category entry points": the situational phrases a buyer would use to describe this need, one set in Chinese and one in English — the two sets of buyers simply use different language.
2. In every piece of material, let the official name and the industry's colloquial name appear together. The category word the buyer says is often completely different from your website headline.
3. Write "what we do / what we don't do / how big the customers we serve are / what price range" as short factual sentences, and use only one form of the entity name — don't call yourself by three different names in the same piece of material.
4. Add a batch of new situational phrases each quarter, picked from the questions that most often stump sales.

**Example**: Byron Sharp of the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute has specifically clarified that "mental availability" means the probability that a buyer notices, recognizes, or thinks of you in a buying situation — far broader than "awareness". Traditional metrics like first mention, recognition, and recall only use prompts stripped of situation (usually just the category name), so they are flawed [official]. In Chinese it is especially easy to grasp: remembering "they can do dust-removal retrofits" and remembering "they're an environmental-equipment company" retrieve two different outcomes. Two other mechanisms point the same way: Hauser & Wernerfelt's evaluation-cost model shows that consideration-set size is a rational buyer's optimum, trading decision cost against marginal benefit, and the model also derives an order-of-entry penalty [official]; Tversky & Kahneman's anchoring experiment shows that the first option to enter the buyer's field of view becomes the frame of reference for every later comparison — a completely irrelevant random starting point can significantly shift judgment (median estimates of 25 and 45 in the two groups) [official].

**Pitfalls**: Just pumping the brand name and keywords. The shortlist remembers "name × situation"; with the situation half missing, the buyer can't retrieve you when they state the need.

## Method 4: Stockpile the Raw Material in the Years Before the Customer Has a Need

**What to do**: Shift part of your budget and time away from "the final push" into public facts that can be retrieved all year round.

**How**

1. First accept the timescale: companies change service providers in banking, legal, software, and telecom about once every 5 years; at any moment only about 20% are in-market within a year, and about 5% in any single quarter.
2. The raw material of the shortlist is piled up bit by bit in the years when the customer feels "this has nothing to do with me", so do this monthly, evaluate it yearly, and don't run it as a campaign.
3. Produce a batch of citable factual material every month — data, specs, definitions, cases — not promotional material.
4. Don't evaluate this kind of spend with an expectation of results in two weeks.

**Example**: The numbers above come from a study by John Dawes of the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute for the LinkedIn B2B Institute, which LinkedIn calls the "95-5 rule": at any moment at most 95% of companies are not in the market for most goods and services; the joint research by the LinkedIn B2B Institute and Ehrenberg-Bass, How B2B Brands Grow, shows that 75% of companies buy computers once every 4 years and 80% change banking services once every 5 years; on the same page there is a jarring contrast — LinkedIn's own survey shows 96% of B2B marketers expect advertising results within two weeks [official]. On the other side is 6sense's annual survey of nearly 4,000 B2B buyers worldwide: in 95% of cases, the vendor that ultimately wins the deal was already on the buyer's "day-one candidate list" before the buyer first contacted sales [vendor claim].

**Pitfalls**: Cutting the shortlist work because "there were no leads this month". What you cut is your ticket into the purchase three or five years from now.

## Method 5: Work All Three Supply Lines at Once

**What to do**: A shortlist has only three supply lines — the corpus you write yourself, the third parties who speak about you, and the default slots a platform presets.

**How**

1. The corpus line: write the facts into an extractable form (those short factual sentences from Method 3). This line you control completely.
2. The third-party line: get other people to speak about you. Overseas it leans on Reddit, YouTube, wiki-style encyclopedias, industry media, and listicles; in Chinese it leans on news sites, industry media, Q&A platforms, and encyclopedia entries that can be publicly indexed. The hard constraint is that you cannot control what a third party says; you can only control "what is worth being said".
3. The default line: get into an entry point's preset slot. Overseas that is the allowlisting application for ChatGPT shopping research; in Chinese it is a platform's admission to its own product pool or supplier pool.
4. This route is an administrative entry, not organic traffic: the work is preparing qualifications, pricing policy, inventory and delivery commitments, and interface capability, then submitting.
5. Judge priority this way: if you can control it, pick the corpus line; if others are willing to speak, pick the third-party line; if the entry point allows you in, pick the default line. If none of the three works, the problem is not customer acquisition — it is your product or your qualifications [our judgment].

**Example**: Ahrefs' December 2025 study (75,000 brands, brand mentions across millions of AI answers) gives a direct correlation ranking: YouTube mentions and branded web-page mentions are strongest; brand mentions overall correlate with AI visibility about 3 times as much as traditional backlinks, while the number of content pages is almost irrelevant [vendor claim]. Another analysis of 680 million citations across ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, and Perplexity says only 11% of domains are cited by both ChatGPT and Perplexity; the three platforms' top sources differ, with ChatGPT favoring Wikipedia (7.8%), and AI Overviews and Perplexity favoring Reddit (2.2% and 6.6% respectively) [vendor claim]. On the Chinese side the default route is already running: ZKH (Chinese: 震坤行)'s "AI product recommendation brain" dynamically optimizes an "exclusive product pool" based on customers' purchase history and usage scenarios, so customers buy directly from its own mall and skip quote comparison — the platform says that in 11 months since launch it recommended more than 20 million lines of materials to customers and drove more than 100 million yuan in revenue growth [vendor claim]. The default value itself has force: Johnson & Goldstein's 2003 study in Science showed that every policy necessarily has a "take no action" default, which imposes a cost on those who need to change their own state; changing the default alone could add thousands of organ donors a year in the United States [official]. On the Chinese side there is one more layer of terrain difference: a 2026 Chinese GEO industry research report judges that AI is turning from an efficiency tool into a high-frequency entry point for information and decisions, with Doubao, DeepSeek, Tencent Yuanbao, and Qwen (Chinese: 千问) contributing most of the growth through ecosystem advantage or vertical scenarios, and more than 40% of users shifting the center of gravity of their search habits from traditional search engines to AI search [third-party reported]. The more the entry points concentrate inside a platform's closed loop, the more the shortlist looks like an admission system; the more they scatter across the open web, the more it looks like a memory contest — on the Chinese industrial-goods side it most resembles the former, and categories like SaaS most resemble the latter: for the same one-line procurement need, the Chinese entry points return Sharecrm (Chinese: 纷享销客), Neocrm (Chinese: 销售易), Wukong CRM (Chinese: 悟空 CRM), Jodoo (Chinese: 简道云), Mingdao Cloud (Chinese: 明道云), and Lark Base (Chinese: 飞书多维表格), while the English entry points return HubSpot, Pipedrive, Zoho, Freshsales, Close, and Salesforce Essentials, with cross-language overlap of 8%–26% [our own measurement]. In regulated industries (finance) the shortlist is generated by the institution's internal model, and the move to get in is compliance admission and the supplier library — content tactics yield little here; education is more direct, with the power to generate the shortlist falling largely to the in-platform handling step — parents ask inside the platform, the platform assigns the inquiry to whoever, and that one is on the list. For shortlists in consumer and local life, more and more are generated by platforms' own recommendation pools and by purchase guidance inside private domains.

**Pitfalls**: Walking only the corpus line and then blaming the abundance of third-party content. Of the three lines you control only one; the other two depend on "having something worth being said" and "being qualified to get through that door".

## Method 6: Manage the Shortlist as "a Roomful of Shortlists"

**What to do**: Get onto each individual shortlist held by each person in the buying group, while being clear that the shortlist itself will be rewritten by others.

**How**

1. First map the buying group: who decides, who uses, who sets requirements, who can veto. In B2B the shortlist is plural — it is stored separately in each member's head, and the versions conflict with one another.
2. Make content for the levels of the buying group; don't only personalize for your point of contact.
3. Treat "I explained it clearly to my contact" as a risk signal — that sentence often falls apart in the bid-evaluation meeting.
4. "What counts as qualified" is also part of the shortlist. If you don't write it clearly, someone else will write it for you.

**Example**: Gartner's sales survey published on 2025-05-07 (632 B2B buyers) shows: 74% of B2B buying teams have "unhealthy conflict" during the decision process; buying groups that reach consensus are 2.5 times more likely to report a "high-quality deal" than groups that don't; and the most counterintuitive set of numbers is that tailoring relevance to the buying-group level has a 20% positive effect on buying-group consensus, while tailoring only at the individual level produces a 59% negative effect on consensus [vendor claim]. There is also a public record of a shortlist being worked in reverse: on January 5, 2026, KNKX and Cascade PBS published ChatGPT conversation records obtained through a public-records request — a city employee in Bellingham, Washington, responsible for replacing the city's billing software, repeatedly asked ChatGPT between January and March 2025 to write solicitation requirements favoring VertexOne, and directly asked it to "exclude Origin SmartCity without explicitly mentioning it"; at least 16 of ChatGPT's generated phrasings appeared verbatim in the final evaluation matrix of 350 requirements. The five-year, $2.7 million contract was ultimately awarded to VertexOne, whose bid was about $2.5 million — about $1 million more than the second-ranked Origin SmartCity at about $1.45 million. The city launched an independent investigation before the media published [third-party reported].

**Pitfalls**: Thinking it's enough that your one contact is on board. Sitting in the bid-evaluation meeting are several other shortlists.

## Notes for this chapter

- The two layers of shortlist must be read separately. The "95-5 rule" and the "day-one candidate list" are about the shortlist in the buyer's head; the measurement in this chapter measures the model-memory layer of the bare API, not an AI search product with live retrieval. Citations at the product layer are indeed highly concentrated, while the model layer is closer to a drifting distribution; this chapter issues no overall judgment that "the shortlist must be long or must be short".
- 6sense's 95% figure comes from the summary page of a vendor-funded survey; the report does not give the full questionnaire and statistical appendix. Here only the directional conclusion is taken; the specific percentage is not asserted before the source is checked [vendor claim].
- The cross-language overlap of 8%–26% is calculated on the basis of "the union of all models' shortlists under the same category"; comparing single answers pairwise would give lower figures. The direction is usable; it is not enough to extrapolate into a precise percentage [our own measurement].
- The correlation coefficients in Method 5 come from vendors' own measurement definitions, not independent reproduction; correlation is not causation. Whether "AI amplifies or weakens the first-mover advantage" has not been measured; the order-of-entry penalty conclusion comes from the era of shelves and advertising.
- Don't take the route of buying your way onto the shortlist. A 2026 CCTV 3·15 exposé of the "AI poisoning" supply chain claimed that GEO-cheating software can generate a dozen-plus fake reviews around a fictitious product within minutes, and get multiple mainstream large models to rank a non-existent product as the top recommendation within 72 hours; in the same month China's first Generative Engine Optimization (GEO) Industry Self-Discipline Convention was signed in Beijing, and the China Advertising Association launched GEO standardization [third-party reported]. This one currently comes relayed via a vendor press release; the specific details must be checked against CCTV's original program and the list of signatories. A bought position is one-off — once you are judged a low-credibility source, all three supply lines are lost at once.
